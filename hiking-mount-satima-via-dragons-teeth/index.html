Satima in the rain

Two days on the Ole Satima trail, from Twin Rocks through Dragon's Teeth to the roof of the Aberdares, with the weather doing what it liked.

By Savanna Guides 17 – 18 September 2026 19.5 km · 634 m up · 4,001 m 8 min read
Direction

From the summit tent

Summit · 4,001 m · Thursday 17 September, 15:45

Journal · Thu 17 Sept · 15:45 · Mount Satima summit

I'm writing this from a tent on the summit of Mount Satima. The plan was to walk from Twin Rocks to the top, drop down the Wandare trail, camp at the Wandare trailhead and return the next day. The weather had other plans.

It has been raining since mid-morning. There were pauses, but never for long. I reached the summit in rain, pitched the tent in rain, ate lunch and made hot tea. It's drizzling as I write.

The trail itself is quite agreeable. The start is gentle, and the views at Dragon's Teeth had me pinching myself.

Filming was harder. Both cameras become dead weight when it rains, so I only shot in the lighter spells.

It will be a cold night. My boots are soaked through and I packed no warm layers.

The back door

Rhino Gate → Twin Rocks · Wednesday 16 September · by road

Journal · Mon 21 Sept · Nanyuki

Mount Satima was my introduction to the northern Aberdares. The drive from Nanyuki to Rhino Gate is uneventful, and the gate itself is modest: a rangers' post and a barrier in need of repair, like a back door into the park.

The drive to Twin Rocks is the better part. Open grassland at the gate gives way to dense forest, then to low moorland shrub near the campsite. The twin rocks appear well before you reach them, as the road narrows and hugs a hillside. They pierce the landscape and announce their presence.

The campsite is hard to miss. A signpost marks the picnic site and the Ole Satima trail, and there is a large parking area. I arrived around 3:30 pm with nobody else about. Facilities are minimal: traces of old campfires and a toilet structure that looked disused.

Rain arrived the moment I started setting up camp. It had been drizzling on the way in; now a stratus cloud covered the whole sky. I cooked dinner under the car's awning with a tarp against the sideways rain, pitched the tent under the awning and called it a day. There was a flicker of 4G, enough for a couple of messages.

For a place known for its cold, the night was pleasant.

A gentle welcome

km 0 → 3 · Thursday 17 September · 08:27 – 09:49 · 3,440 → 3,553 m

Journal · Mon 21 Sept, continued

An early start was the plan. In practice, breakfast, packing and loading the car took longer than they should have, and I set off around half past eight.

The Mount Satima trail welcomes you gently. A mostly flat 4x4 track gives you time to settle into the walk. Where the track ends, the climb through the moorland begins and the first jagged rocks hint at what lies ahead. It's worth turning around here: Twin Rocks and the campsite already look different from above.

After the initial climb the route drops into a small valley, crosses a tiny stream and heads up a steeper slope. Nipple Rock stands in the middle ground, with Dragon's Teeth behind it.

Barely an hour in, the clouds gathered. A drizzle became a proper downpour. Away went the cameras and out came the rain jacket. I met the first hikers of the day, a couple and their guide, who went on ahead.

Dragon's Teeth, in the rain

km 3 → 6.75 · 09:49 – 11:58 · 3,553 → 3,710 m

Journal · Mon 21 Sept, continued

It's a slow march through the rain to Dragon's Teeth, the ground softening underfoot. Another group passed me on the way. We met briefly at the rocks before they carried on and I stopped for a snack in a short break in the rain.

Under a grey sky the outcrops have a sombre presence above the wet grass. I was cold and wet and very glad to be there. Only on the way back did I see how much of the formation the cloud had hidden.

Zooming out

km 6.75 → 9.5 · 11:58 – 13:26 · 3,710 → 3,938 m

Journal · Mon 21 Sept, continued

Past the break, the trail climbs and then drops through a gap in the rocks, almost like a gate, and open moorland returns. The path snakes gently through the grass, and the further you go the more of Dragon's Teeth you see. The view zooms out as you walk.

On a gentle ridge the rain returned, heavier this time, and I was soaked from the knees down. Another couple passed me; with a full pack of camping gear I was slower still. I caught them further up, sheltering on an exposed ridge with nothing to shelter behind, and pushed on. This was the summit leg.

Four thousand and one

km 9.5 → 10 · 13:26 – 13:57 · 4,001 m

Journal · Mon 21 Sept, continued

I met a group coming down and, in a lull, the couple from earlier at the base of the summit. Then the rain returned hard and I climbed the last stretch with it pouring around me, reaching the summit just before two in the afternoon.

A few celebratory photos, then the question of where to camp. A gently sloping patch beside a lone giant lobelia would do. I pitched the tent as frozen droplets came down all around.

A cold night, with wet boots and no warm layers. Hot tea had rarely been so welcome.

The tent sat beside the lone lobelia, a few steps below the summit sign. The rain thinned to a drizzle by evening and, somewhere in the dark, stopped.

A different world

km 10.5 → 19.5 · Friday 18 September · 08:24 – 11:52 · back to 3,440 m

Journal · Sat 26 Sept · 06:25

Coming down the following day was a joy. The weather stayed pleasant, the walking was mostly downhill and the rain cover stayed packed away.

Dragon's Teeth looked different from the opposite direction. With time to linger I could properly take in the shapes of the rocks and the spaces between them. It felt like being transported to a different world.

Camping at Twin Rocks

A flat patch and a signpost

Signposted as the Twin-Rock Picnic Site and Ole Satima Hiking Trail, with a large parking area and room for a tent or an awning. Facilities are minimal and the toilet structure looked disused. The trail starts right there.

Guide

Not required

I hiked without one. The trail is signposted and clear in all but thick cloud. The other groups I met were guided, which is a fine choice rather than a rule. Check current arrangements with the park.

No guide required · Solo-friendly

Gear that failed

Learn from my feet

Boots soaked through by the ridge: bring gaiters or properly waterproof footwear. Pack warm layers for a 4,000 m night and keep sleeping gear dry. Cameras become dead weight in long rain; a phone in a dry bag does more.

Cell signal

A flicker of 4G

Intermittent 4G at Twin Rocks campsite, enough to send a few messages. Don't count on it higher up.
